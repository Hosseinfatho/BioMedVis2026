**StateDriver** summarizes which **non-local inter-residue contacts** (heavy-atom minimum distance, sequence separation ≥ 3) and **residue–ligand proximities** characterize a selected Markov state, and how **Free Aβ42**, **Aβ42 + TMP**, and **Aβ42 + SPA** differ. Offline preprocess (`state_driver_interactions.json`) aggregates per-frame min heavy-atom distances into a Best–Hummer-style soft-contact score; at runtime the **hill-and-wave chain** (R1–R42) shows clickable residue nodes, and up to 14 strongest arcs encode three channels: **arc bulge (length)** → proximity (closer pairs bulge more), **arc width** → frequency/occupancy (`mean_soft`), and **arc opacity** → persistence/consistency (`1/(1 + distance_std)`). When multiple systems are active, arc **color** is differential: blue (Free), green (TMP), orange (SPA) if that condition wins the contact, and **gray** if strengths agree within ε = 0.03 (shared); arcs above vs below the chain are layout only, not drug-specific lanes. Below the chain, **TMP/SPA rows** plot top residue–ligand contacts as sized glyphs under residue numbers (Free has no ligand row). Clicking an **arc** highlights both residues in Viewer3D and loads a representative frame from the winning system; clicking a **residue node** filters arcs to that residue; clicking a **ligand marker** highlights the residue in ligand context—all linking abstract state-level contact statistics to 3D structure.
